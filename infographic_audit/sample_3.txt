```mermaid
flowchart TD
  subgraph Phase1 ["🌱 Stage 1: Setup & Data Prep"]

  subgraph Phase2 ["⚙️ Stage 2: Divide & Conquer Engine"]
    direction TB
    E["⚡ <b>Compute Midpoint Index</b><br/><i>mid = low + (high - low) / 2</i>"]
    F{{"❓ <b>Compare Arr[mid] vs Target</b><br/><i>Evaluate relative order</i>"}}
    G["✂️ <b>Discard Left Half</b><br/><i>Target > mid ➔ low = mid + 1</i>"]
    H["✂️ <b>Discard Right Half</b><br/><i>Target < mid ➔ high = mid - 1</i>"]
    I{{"❓ <b>Valid Range Exists?</b><br/><i>Check if low <= high</i>"}}

    E -->|"Evaluates position"| F
    F -->|"If Arr[mid] < Target"| G
    F -->|"If Arr[mid] > Target"| H
    G --> I
    H --> I
    I -->|"Yes: Loop Continues"| E
  end

  subgraph Phase3 ["🚀 Stage 3: Outcomes & Efficiency"]
    J(["🏆 <b>Element Located</b><br/><i>Returns index position</i>"])
    K(["❌ <b>Element Missing</b><br/><i>Returns -1 / null flag</i>"])
    L["📊 <b>Logarithmic Performance</b><br/><i>Time: O(log N) • Space: O(1)</i>"]
  end

  C ==>|"Sets low=0, high=N-1"| E
  D ==>|"Input to loop"| E
  F ==>|"If Arr[mid] == Target"| J
  I -->|"No: Range Exhausted"| K
  J --- L
  K --- L
```

Key Takeaways:
- **Prerequisite Order**: The array must be pre-sorted; binary search relies on sorted order to determine which half of data to eliminate.
- **Divide-and-Conquer**: Each comparison cuts the active search space in half by inspecting the median element (`mid`).
- **Logarithmic Efficiency**: Achieves $O(\log N)$ time complexity, allowing searches through 1,000,000 items in ~20 steps compared to $O(N)$ linear scans.
- **Memory Efficiency**: Operates iteratively with $O(1)$ auxiliary space complexity, updating pointer boundaries (`low`, `high`) in-place.
